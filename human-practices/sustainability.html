Sustainability

PAGER-Yeast is an engineered-yeast sensor for influenza A. For this project, sustainability means keeping the system usable where influenza is most often missed — clinics without laboratory infrastructure, schools, border checkpoints and homes — and keeping the parts reusable by other teams. Three design decisions follow from that: a low-cost portable readout, a recognition module that can be re-targeted without new hardware, and an open, standardised parts set.

Designed for low-resource settings

The reporter is read as fluorescence intensity, which a portable fluorimeter can measure; the workflow does not require a qPCR thermocycler or an ELISA plate reader. The intended deployment settings are primary-care clinic screening, school morning checks, border quarantine ports and at-home self-testing — places where a result has to arrive quickly and at low cost. A semi-quantitative relationship between fluorescence intensity and viral load is being established through DCZ concentration-gradient and induction-time optimisation, so that a reader without calibration curves can still return an interpretable result.

A recognition module that is swapped, not rebuilt

The membrane anchor — the α-factor secretion signal and the linker scaffold — stays fixed, and only the VHH sequence is replaced to target a new antigen. Re-targeting the sensor to H5, H7, SARS-CoV-2 or RSV therefore reuses the same chassis, the same reporter and the same reader: extending the analyte range costs a VHH sequence, not a new instrument. The downstream GPCR signal rewiring and the dual-mode reporter are likewise kept universal across targets.

Open parts and reproducible protocols

The signalling and reporter components are being submitted as iGEM Registry-compatible parts, together with the protocols and plasmid maps needed to reproduce each construct. Standardised, registry-indexed parts let other teams reuse the module instead of re-optimising it, and keep the work auditable.

Limits of this assessment

This page states design intent and the reuse strategy; it is not a life-cycle assessment. The material footprint of the reader, the disposal route of the used test components and the energy demand of long-term field deployment have not been measured yet, and no environmental benefit is claimed on their behalf.
