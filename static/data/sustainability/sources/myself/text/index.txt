 Future Vision: From H1N1 to the Broad Spectrum Platform

 Once the H1N1 proof of concept is complete, the core value of the PAGER-Yeast platform lies in its
 modularity and replaceability: the membrane nanobody recognition unit can be rapidly iterated against
 new antigens, while the downstream GPCR signal-rewiring and the dual-mode reporter system stay
 universal. Combined with a low-cost portable fluorescence reader, the system could move from a
 laboratory prototype to a field-ready testing tool for primary-care settings.

 Modular recognition unit: keeping the α-factor secretion anchor and linker
 scaffold, swapping only the VHH sequence would extend coverage to H5, H7 and other influenza
 subtypes — and potentially to respiratory pathogens such as SARS-CoV-2 and RSV;

 Signal optimization: by tuning the DCZ concentration gradient and induction
 time, we plan to correlate fluorescence intensity with viral load for semi-quantitative detection
 that matches portable fluorimeters;

 Multi-scenario use: aimed at settings that need fast, low-cost results —
 primary-care clinic screening, school morning checks, border quarantine ports and at-home
 self-testing;

 Open source &amp; standardization: submitting standardized biological parts
 (promoters, reporter genes, signaling modules) in an iGEM Registry-compatible form, together with
 reproducible protocols and plasmid maps.
