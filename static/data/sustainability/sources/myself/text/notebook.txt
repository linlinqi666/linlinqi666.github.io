 Notebook

 These notebooks serve as the main entry for our wet-lab documentation of the yeast-based biosensor targeting airborne influenza A virus (IAV). Our PAGER (Protein-fragment Assisted GPCR Expression Reporter) antigen-gated receptor system is built in four independent functional modules. Below you can access experimental records for chassis engineering, humanized G-protein chimera construction, PAGER fusion-receptor assembly, and reporter-module validation respectively. Each record links to the Protocol page, and to external resources cited inside the document.

 Chassis Engineering

 This notebook records wet-lab procedures for chassis optimization of our yeast biosensor. We performed CRISPR-Cas9-mediated knockout of endogenous FAR1 and SST2 in Saccharomyces cerevisiae BY4741, aiming to eliminate cell-cycle arrest and G-protein-signaling negative feedback, so as to improve signal sensitivity for our downstream PAGER antigen-gated receptor system.

 Humanized G-Protein Chimera

 This notebook records the humanization engineering of GPA1 in Saccharomyces cerevisiae — replacing the native C-terminal KIGII motif with the human Gαq EYNLV sequence to retune G-protein coupling for the PAGER receptor.

 PAGER Fusion Receptor

 This notebook records the construction of the PAGER antigen-gated system for Influenza A virus detection — assembly of the membrane-anchored scFv–Gα–Gγ fusion receptor, yeast transformation, expression validation, and HA-antigen-induced activation detection.
